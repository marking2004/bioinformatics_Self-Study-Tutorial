Lab exercises

Hands-on labs organised as "goal → setup → procedure → reading the results → exercises", mapped one-to-one onto the modules above. Every tool and database links to its official site.

  • Environment

    Lab 1: The Linux Command Line and File Management

    Bioinformatics almost always runs on Linux. This lab walks the terminal, the file system, pipes and redirection, drills the dozen commands you use every day, and installs your first tool.

  • Programming

    Lab 2: R Basics and Data Visualisation

    R is the workhorse of downstream omics analysis. This lab covers data structures, file I/O, installing from CRAN and Bioconductor, and drawing your first presentable figure with ggplot2.

  • Programming

    Lab 3: Python Basics and Sequence Extraction

    Sequences are text, and Python handles text well. This lab parses FASTA and GenBank with Biopython, pulls sequences by ID, computes GC content and reverse complements, and writes results in bulk.

  • Databases

    Lab 4: Sequence Databases and Record Formats

    The same sequence is packaged differently in different databases. This lab reads a GenBank record field by field, separates primary from secondary databases, and teaches field-limited querying.

  • Databases

    Lab 5: Retrieving Sequences and Raw Reads

    Data can be clicked or pulled. This lab does both: batch sequence retrieval with E-utilities, raw read download with the SRA Toolkit, alternative sourcing from ENA/DDBJ, and integrity checks.

  • Sequence analysis

    Lab 6: Pairwise Alignment and BLAST Search

    BLAST is the most used and most misread tool in bioinformatics. This lab covers choosing the right program and database, and reading E-value, identity and coverage together.

  • Sequence analysis

    Lab 7: Multiple Sequence Alignment and Visualisation

    Stacking homologs reveals which positions evolution kept. This lab aligns with MAFFT, dereplicates, trims and visualises an alignment, then judges whether the result can be trusted.

  • Sequence analysis

    Lab 8: Building and Evaluating a Phylogeny

    Between an alignment and a publishable tree sit model selection and support values. This lab runs the full route with IQ-TREE and explains branch lengths, support, and why a gene tree is not a species tree.

  • Genomics

    Lab 9: Sequence Assembly and Gene Prediction

    Sequencing gives you millions of short reads; you must assemble them before you can locate genes. This lab runs QC, assembly, evaluation, gene prediction and ORF finding, and explains how far to trust the output.

  • Genomics

    Lab 10: Genome Visualisation and Browser Work

    Genomic data only makes sense on coordinates. This lab uses Ensembl, UCSC and local IGV to inspect gene structure, coverage and variants, and nails down coordinates and genome versions.

  • Transcriptomics

    Lab 11: RNA-seq from QC to Enrichment

    Transcriptomics is the omics analysis undergraduates meet most. This lab runs the full chain: QC, alignment or alignment-free quantification, counting, differential expression and enrichment — plus the usual trip-ups: FPKM, replicates and batch effects.

  • Multi-omics

    Lab 12: Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA)

    Differential expression looks at single genes; WGCNA looks at modules of genes. This lab builds a network with the R package WGCNA, detects modules, relates them to traits and picks hub genes.

  • Transcriptomics

    Lab 13: Non-coding RNA — miRNA Discovery, Target Prediction and ceRNA Networks

    miRNA calls rest on two kinds of evidence: sequence and structure. Target prediction is inherently noisy. This lab runs discovery, prediction, curated-database checking and ceRNA construction, and says which claims hold up.

  • Multi-omics

    Lab 14: Single-cell Transcriptome Analysis

    Single-cell work replaces a tissue average with per-cell resolution. This lab runs Seurat through QC, dimensionality reduction, clustering, markers and cell-type annotation, and flags doublets, batch effects and resolution choices.

  • Protein structure

    Lab 15: Protein Domains, Structure Prediction and Homology Modelling

    Given a sequence, catalogue its domains first, then choose how to get a structure. This lab scans domains, checks transmembrane regions and signal peptides, models with SWISS-MODEL and AlphaFold, and reads the quality metrics.

  • Drug design

    Lab 16: Molecular Docking and Binding-mode Analysis

    Docking asks whether a small molecule fits a pocket and how. This lab prepares receptor and ligand, defines the pocket, docks with Vina, analyses the binding mode, and stresses what a scoring function can and cannot tell you.

  • Multi-omics

    Lab 17: Quantitative Proteomics

    Mass spectrometry yields spectra; a search engine turns them into proteins and quantities. This lab covers data retrieval, database search, filtering, normalisation and differential analysis — and why missing values must not be set to zero.

  • Networks

    Lab 18: Network Construction and Analysis with Cytoscape

    Genes, proteins and interactions from earlier labs end up as a network. This lab builds one in Cytoscape, runs topological analysis, finds modules and hubs, and covers figure export that survives peer review.
